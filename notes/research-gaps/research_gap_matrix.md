## Methodological Synthesis

The closest studies establish different parts of the proposed method and must not be conflated:

| Study | Retrieval-level strategy comparison | Downstream scientific QA / evidence grounding |
|---|---|---|
| Solar Intelligence | Uses BM25+dense/RRF in a solar corpus; no BM25-only vs dense-only vs hybrid comparison | Solar QA examples and displayed citations; no formal citation attribution benchmark |
| Lit3R | Hybrid pipeline and retrieval evaluation; no isolated three-way BM25/dense/hybrid strategy experiment | Strong scientific QA overlap; paper/evidence precision, recall, and F1 and answer metrics |
| SQuAI | Sparse+dense hybrid pipeline; no established three-way controlled comparison | Scientific QA, citations, faithfulness/relevance evaluation |
| IRPAPERS | **Yes: directly compares BM25-only, Arctic 2.0 dense-only, and hybrid text retrieval on the same scientific-page corpus/question benchmark** | QA compares TextRAG/ImageRAG, modality, and retrieval depth; it does not run BM25-only, dense-only, and hybrid as three same-generator QA arms or evaluate citation attribution |
| OpenScholar | Dense retriever plus external API/web candidate sources and reranking; no BM25-only/dense-only/hybrid arms | Strong multi-domain scientific synthesis QA and sentence-level citation precision/recall/F1, but no solar/PV corpus |
| RILS | System-level conditions include BM25+Generation, standard dense RAG, and hybrid BM25+dense RILS; 900 common assessment queries span six technologies, including 150 solar-PV queries | Downstream answer/system metrics and NLI claim verification are reported; retrieval is not isolated as the only changed factor, and same generator/prompt/citation metrics across arms are not established |
| PeerQA | Compares BM25, dense, multi-vector, and sparse models for evidence retrieval; no matched hybrid arm | Scientific QA, answerability, and generation are evaluated separately; downstream RAG uses SPLADEv3 contexts, not matched retrieval-model QA arms |
| Solar GenAI | Solar-PV dense retrieval is implemented; no retrieval-strategy comparison | LLM-only vs RAG and answer/hallucination observations; no formal citation attribution metric |
| HySemRAG | Semantic/keyword/graph components are combined in a scientific-literature synthesis pipeline; no matched retriever arms | Citation verification and literature synthesis are addressed, but no controlled downstream BM25/dense/hybrid comparison |
| Wang et al. (multi-level evidence synthesis) | Abstract describes multi-level retrieval and cross-document scientific QA experiments; retrieval arms/controls are unknown because full text is unavailable | Potentially relevant system/QA comparator; matched retriever-only downstream conditions and citation/evidence scoring are not established from abstract |
| Han et al. (SLR automation with RAG) | No author-run retrieval comparison; BM25/dense methods occur as background and in a proposed framework | Review/framework context only; no evaluated downstream QA or evidence/citation outcomes |

1. **Retrieval-level comparison:** IRPAPERS directly compares BM25-only, dense-only, and hybrid retrieval over a scientific-paper corpus under a common question benchmark. The proposed work must not present that retrieval-level contrast itself as unestablished.
2. **Downstream RAG/QA comparison:** PeerQA evaluates multiple evidence retrievers, answerability, and generation, but downstream generation uses SPLADEv3 contexts rather than matched BM25/dense/hybrid QA arms. OpenScholar provides scientific synthesis and citation evaluation, not the three retriever arms.
3. **Energy and solar/PV:** RILS is a close system-level comparator: BM25+Generation, standard dense RAG, and hybrid RILS are compared on a 900-query renewable-energy benchmark including a 150-query solar-PV subset. The system bundles differ beyond retrieval, and a same-generator/prompt-controlled three-retriever comparison with downstream citation/evidence metrics is not established. Solar Intelligence and Solar GenAI provide solar RAG application precedents without the three-way retriever experiment.
4. **Evidence-grounded QA:** Lit3R, PeerQA, and OpenScholar provide scientific QA/evidence or citation metrics; SQuAI provides scientific QA with citation/support analysis. Their retrieval configurations do not isolate the proposed three strategies as matched downstream QA arms.
5. **Remaining question:** Whether retrieval strategy changes downstream answer and evidence-grounding outcomes under controlled conditions—especially over a solar/PV scientific-paper corpus—remains distinct from whether retrieval metrics differ and from RILS's system-level comparison. No field-wide claim is made here.

Wang et al. is the outstanding registered full-text uncertainty: the abstract establishes a closely related scientific-literature QA/RAG task but does not establish the controlled retrieval arms or outcome measures. Han et al. is no longer unresolved: its full text is a review/framework and provides no author-run experiment matching the candidate design.
